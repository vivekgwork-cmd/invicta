What are Advanced Placement exams?
Advanced Placement, or AP, is a College Board programme offering college-level courses and exams to school students.
Each exam assesses a specific subject, allowing you to demonstrate knowledge beyond your regular school curriculum. Universities decide how they use AP courses and scores in admissions, credit and placement.
Whether you’re exploring engineering, economics, computer science or the humanities, AP can help you investigate a subject before choosing your degree.


Why consider AP?
Show academic ambition
Challenging coursework can give universities more context about your academic interests and readiness. Strong AP results may support your application where the institution considers them.
Explore a future major
Enjoy mathematics? Curious about economics? Interested in coding? AP lets you experience the demands of a subject before committing to it at university.
Build university study skills
Develop analytical thinking, independent study, time management and clear communication through demanding subject work.
Explore credit and placement opportunities
Some universities award credit or let you move beyond introductory courses for qualifying scores. The required score and the benefit differ by institution and subject.
Create more room in your degree
Where AP credit counts towards your degree, it may create space for electives or advanced study. Any savings in time or tuition depend on your university’s rules and degree requirements.
Challenge yourself with a purpose
Take on a subject because it fits your interests and goals. A manageable combination of relevant exams is more useful than a long list without a clear reason.

AP Calculus AB and BC
Build a foundation for quantitative study.
AP Calculus AB introduces limits, differentiation, integration and their applications. It develops your ability to interpret mathematical relationships and solve problems using calculus.
AP Calculus BC includes the AB foundation and extends into additional topics such as parametric and polar functions, further integration techniques, and sequences and series.
Both can support students exploring mathematics, engineering, physical sciences and other quantitative fields. The right choice depends on your existing foundation and the time you can devote to preparation.

AP Physics C
Understand the physics. Show the reasoning.
Physics C consists of two separate courses and exams, both using calculus to explore physical systems.
Mechanics covers motion, forces, energy, momentum, rotation and oscillations. Preparation develops mathematical reasoning and the ability to connect concepts with physical situations.
Electricity and Magnetism explores electric fields, circuits, magnetic fields and electromagnetic induction.
These subjects are especially relevant to students considering engineering or physical sciences. A suitable calculus foundation is important.

AP Microeconomics and Macroeconomics
Learn to make sense of economic decisions.
Microeconomics focuses on individuals, firms and markets. Explore supply and demand, production costs, competition, factor markets and the role of government.
Macroeconomics examines the economy as a whole. Study national income, growth, unemployment, inflation, fiscal policy and monetary policy.
Both develop your ability to use graphs, models and evidence to explain economic outcomes. They are separate exams, so you can plan for one or both.

Know how your exam will be delivered
Fully digital exams
For AP Computer Science A and AP English Language and Composition, students complete both sections in Bluebook.
Hybrid digital exams
For the Calculus, Physics C and Economics exams listed here, students complete multiple-choice questions and view free-response prompts in Bluebook, then write their free-response answers in paper booklets.
Your school or test centre will explain device requirements and exam-day arrangements. Practise in the relevant format before your exam.

Frequently asked questions
When are AP exams held?
AP exams are held annually in May. Each subject has a scheduled date, and your coordinator or test centre confirms the time and location.
How long does an AP exam take?
The duration varies by subject. The exams listed on this page range from 2 hours 10 minutes to 3 hours 15 minutes, excluding breaks and administrative time.
How do I register?
Registration is arranged through a participating school or authorised test centre. Contact the AP coordinator early to confirm availability, payment and deadlines. A College Board account alone does not reserve your exam.
Can I take an AP exam if my school does not offer the course?
You may be able to take the exam at a school or centre that accepts external students. Contact possible centres early to confirm whether they can accommodate you.
How much does an AP exam cost?
The amount payable varies by school or authorised centre. Ask for the total exam fee, administration charges and any late fees before registering. Coaching fees are separate.
Are there late or cancellation fees?
Additional charges may apply. Your coordinator or test centre can explain registration deadlines, cancellation rules and any applicable charges.
Is there a penalty for incorrect multiple-choice answers?
Incorrect multiple-choice answers do not attract a guessing penalty. Timed practice can help you make informed choices and use your time effectively.
Will AP guarantee university admission or credit?
No. Each institution sets its own admissions and credit policies. AP should support your broader academic plan.
When will I receive my scores?
Scores are generally released in July through your College Board account. Check the official release information for your exam year.
How do I send scores to universities?
Use College Board’s AP score-sending service. Check the current free score-send deadline and any charges for additional reports.
How many AP exams should I take?
There is no single ideal number. Choose a manageable set that fits your interests, school workload and university goals.
